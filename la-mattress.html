What happens during a mattress clean, step by step?

Your cleaner starts with the label and the mattress type, pocket sprung, memory foam, latex or a child's cot mattress, and picks a method to suit the mattress. The mattress is vacuumed first with a strong machine along the seams, edges and tufting, where most dust mites and skin flakes gather. Every mark is then treated on its own before the whole surface is cleaned and sanitised, with a dust mite treatment and an odour treatment where needed.

Moisture is kept to a minimum throughout. You'll see some firms advertise mattresses cleaned by hot water extraction, but that can leave the filling damp, and wet cleaning may damage memory foam. Choosing between hot water extraction or the dry method is one of the first decisions your cleaner makes; some companies rely on a dry powder method for everything, while we use a dry method to suit each mattress, or a low moisture clean on sprung ones.

Can old sweat and urine stains come out of a mattress?

In most cases they come out or fade a great deal. Sweat rings, urine, blood, tea and pet accidents all respond well, and the sooner a stain is treated, the better the result. Your cleaner looks at each mark first and tells you plainly what to expect, because a stain that's years old, or one already scrubbed with bleach or a household spray, may not come out completely.

Smells are treated at the source rather than masked with perfume. If an accident happened recently, let us know when you book, so we come prepared with the right treatment. If you suspect bed bugs, call pest control first; we can clean once the treatment is done.
