How does professional carpet cleaning work?

Your carpet cleaner checks the fibre and how dirty each area is first, because a busy hallway and a spare bedroom rarely need the same treatment. The carpet is vacuumed thoroughly, each stain is pre treated, and then we clean by hot water extraction, often called steam cleaning, or use a low moisture method for carpets that shouldn't get too wet.

Hot water extraction rinses the pile and draws the dirty water back out in one pass, so no sticky residue is left to attract new dirt. Smells get their own treatment where needed, and the pile is groomed at the end so it dries evenly and looks fresh.

Can carpet cleaning remove pet stains, mud and odours?

Most everyday stains lift completely or fade a great deal: mud, tea, coffee, wine, food and the traffic lanes on stairs and hallways. Pet accidents are treated at the source with an odour treatment, and the cleaning removes much of the dust, pet hair and allergens held deep in the carpet.

Some marks, such as bleach spots, dyes, ink or stains already scrubbed with a shop bought spray, may only partly fade. We look at every stain before we start and tell you honestly what to expect. For fresh mud from a country park walk, let it dry fully and vacuum it before we arrive, because wet mud spreads when it's rubbed.

How long does carpet take to dry after cleaning?

Most carpets are dry within a few hours. Drying time depends on the carpet, the weather and the airflow in the room, so open a window or keep the heating on low, especially on damp winter days. Walk on it in clean socks if you need to, and keep pets off until it's dry.

Before we arrive, clear small items and anything fragile from the floor. We can move light furniture, and heavier pieces are cleaned around. Let us know what caused any stains, as that helps us choose the right treatment.
